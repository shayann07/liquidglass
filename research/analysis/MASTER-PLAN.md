12 September 2026. Prepared for an implementer who cannot consult the author. This document contains the reasoning, experiments, branch decisions, shader specification and acceptance contract. It contains no new fitted result. No numerical optimization, new rendering, build or device test was performed in preparing it. Only this document was written; both repositories were read-only. Their checked branches were research/ios27-measurements and feat/liquidglass-0.2-measured, and both git status outputs were empty.

**0. The objective and the limit of the requested proof.** Recover the smallest jointly predictive image-formation model supported by the corpus, and compile that model into the existing library. Attempt to recover height, index and source gap jointly. Report an equivalence class when they are not independently identifiable. Do not assume an index and subsequently describe it as recovered. Do not claim that rejecting one lens proves that no optical system could produce the pictures. Finite raster observations cannot exclude every arbitrarily curved, multiply reflecting or multilayer optical system. They can exclude specified classes, often decisively. If the excluded classes include every inexpensive, shared model defined here, ship the explicit compositor described below and report that conclusion at its proper scope.

There are two distinct successful outcomes: a physically constrained model predicts withheld measurements; or numerical tests reject that model class and establish a simpler predictive compositor. Neither outcome requires pretending to know Apple's implementation. A physically named curve with independent corrections for each measurement is a failed outcome.

The corpus is large, but it is not 45,000 independent, fully observed ray maps. Black areas have no displacement information. A grey ramp gives one coordinate only, and only after removing tone. Periodic stripes give phase modulo their period. Some frames contain unknown app content, different source layers, changing dimming or transforms. Consecutive frames and target twins often repeat the same observation. More rows in a rank-deficient inverse problem do not remove its null space. Sections 4 and 10 specify how to discover, rather than assume, the actual rank.

**3. Measurement coordinates and why inversion requires a likelihood.** Use image coordinates p=(x,y) with pixel centers at integer coordinates, y increasing downwards. Geometry is continuous. An edge at e has depth d=e-y for a horizontal bottom edge; m=gradient(d)=(0,-1) is inward. For a general shape d is Euclidean signed distance positive inside and m is its inward unit gradient. Let q(p) be the source position in screen-aligned backdrop coordinates before the source-to-texture transform. Define Delta=s-d, where s is source depth relative to the same edge. Then q=p+Delta m; at a bottom edge q_y=y-Delta. Positive Delta samples inward and moves a displayed feature toward that bottom edge. This removes the recurring ambiguity between sampling direction and apparent image motion.

Let source layer j have known or independently measured affine transform T_j(t), from its native coordinates to screen coordinates. Sample its texture at z_j=T_j(t)^(-1) q_j(p,t). For the wallpaper, source, target and display coordinates are not interchangeable. A first guess for Home Screen registration is scale 1.1375 and y offset -290, but E0 re-estimates it outside glass. For the held bar, T includes 1.05 whole-bar and 1.18 selected-item transforms only for the layer to which each applies.

Let the observed digital channel be Y_c(p,t). A useful observation equation is

    Y = EncodeAndCapture { Composite[ source responses, masks, transmission, reflection, foreground ] } + noise.

Do not invert Y as though it were an unmodified source channel. On a neutral affine ramp B(x)=m_r x+b_r, m_r=255/1169=0.218135 levels/px, a normalized symmetric source blur preserves the ramp away from its boundaries. If Y=a B(q_x)+b, then

    q_x = ((Y-b)/a - b_r)/m_r,
    dq_x/dY = 1/(a m_r).

Even at a=1, one level means 4.584 px. At a=0.55 it means 8.34 px. A spatially varying tint, reflection, clipping or support boundary invalidates that direct inversion. Propagate uncertainty in Y,a,b and source registration; use the ramp as a weak one-coordinate constraint unless its recovered uncertainty is demonstrably smaller. For a stripe, retain candidate phases q modulo period, and for a flat region mark displacement unobserved. An observation may constrain a projection of q without constraining its full vector.

A local linearization gives delta Y approximately a gradient(B)^T delta q. The information matrix is proportional to the sum of gradient(B) gradient(B)^T over the measurement patch. Rank zero means no mapping information; rank one means the aperture problem; two separated positive eigenvalues support a two-dimensional match. Require lambda_min/lambda_max >=0.05 for a reported unconstrained 2D point. Otherwise store a normal/projection constraint, not a fabricated 2D vector.

**4. Derive the optical null model, including its non-uniqueness.** The camera is initially orthographic, viewing in direction v=(0,0,-1), through air into a homogeneous body of index n>1. Its top is z=h(x,y), its flat underside z=0, and the source plane z=-g with g>=0. The ray is traced backwards from the camera. All lengths use the same virtual units, initially reference-image pixels. The silhouette does not measure h. Projected lens proudness is an xy measurement, not z height.

The outward top normal is N=(-h_x,-h_y,1)/sqrt(1+|gradient h|²). For any incident unit direction v and normal N pointing into its incident medium, set c=-N dot v, eta=n_incident/n_transmitted and k=1-eta²(1-c²). Conservation of the tangential wave-vector gives transmitted tangential component eta(v+cN); normalization supplies the normal component -sqrt(k)N. Therefore

    t = eta v + (eta c - sqrt(k)) N.

If k<0 there is no transmitted direction. The reflected direction is r=v-2(v dot N)N, obtained by reversing only the normal component. These equations give vector Snell/reflection, including which side of an interface the normal must face. Do not clamp k to zero and call the result transmission. A tiny negative numerical error within 1e-7 may be clamped; physically negative k selects a different path.

In a normal cross-section, set p_h=h'(d), alpha=atan(p_h), beta=asin(sin(alpha)/n), theta=alpha-beta. The inside ray has inward horizontal component sin(theta), downward component -cos(theta). It reaches the flat bottom after vertical travel h, hence inward shift h tan(theta). At the bottom, sin(gamma)=n sin(theta); source travel adds g tan(gamma). Thus

    Delta = h tan(theta) + g tan(gamma),       s=d+Delta.

This assumes the ray stays within the body and reaches that bottom before another surface. The outer sidewall and a nonplanar lower surface are treated explicitly in the extension below.

A form without trigonometric functions is useful both for inversion and shaders. Put P=p_h and D=sqrt(n²+(n²-1)P²). Using sin(alpha)=P/sqrt(1+P²) and the tangent subtraction formula gives

    k_t = tan(theta) = P(D-1)/(D+P²),
    k_b = tan(gamma) = n k_t / sqrt(1-(n²-1)k_t²),
    Delta = h k_t + g k_b.

The bottom transmission exists only if 1-(n²-1)k_t²>0. A bounded single-interface k_t does not establish a bounded two-interface transfer; k_b grows toward grazing exit. For n=1.5 the top slope at this bottom critical condition is P=1/(sqrt(n²-1)-1) approximately 8.47, alpha approximately 83.3 degrees. If n<=sqrt(2), that particular critical angle is not reached before a grazing top incident ray. These are model statements, not recovered indices.

For small |P|, alpha≈P, beta≈P/n, theta≈(1-1/n)P and gamma≈(n-1)P. Therefore

    Delta ≈ (n-1)(h/n+g) h'.

In 2D this is q=p+gradient(Psi), with

    Psi = (n-1)(h²/(2n)+g h).

This potential is a useful cheap inverse representation, but it is not a proof of physical glass. Given measured Delta(d), define Psi(d)=Psi(d0)+integral Delta(v) dv. For ANY chosen n>1, g>=0 and admissible integration constant, a height producing that same paraxial map is

    h(d) = -n g + sqrt(n²g² + 2n Psi(d)/(n-1)).

Choose the constant so h is positive. This is an explicit family of indistinguishable heights, indices and gaps. Repeated frames constrain Psi more accurately; they do not select one member. A uniform rescaling in z combined with changed index can preserve the same paraxial power. Known xy radii do not supply a measurement of z.

The ambiguity is not solely a small-angle artifact. Write the exact transfer as F(h,h',n,g)=Delta. Where partial F/partial h' is nonzero and the transmitted path remains valid, implicit inversion yields h'=G(Delta,h,n,g). For nearby choices of n,g and initial h(d0), this first-order ODE has nearby local solutions with identical Delta. Extra boundary conditions or cross-role profile restrictions may remove some of those freedoms. If the fit identifies n only after imposing one shared two-coefficient profile, call that identification conditional on that profile. Profile likelihood across n,g, alternative height bases and roles is mandatory.

There is no universal no-surface proof from a failed optimizer. A valid rejection statement names its index range, height family, boundary conditions, path count, fit tolerances and searched parameter domain. The positive-cap sign and single-branch continuity tests below are stronger: they apply to entire classes under their stated assumptions.

**5. Folds, tangential stretch and the two sign tests.** For q=p+Delta(d)m, J_n=ds/dd=1+Delta'. A source interval ds occupies display interval dd=ds/J_n, so positive J_n<1 magnifies, J_n>1 compresses, and J_n<0 reverses. At J_n=0 the inverse image of a source landmark can branch; the forward q(p) remains continuous. The small-slope surface above yields

    J_n = 1+(n-1)[(h/n+g)h''+(h')²/n].

A sufficiently negative shoulder curvature h'' can create a fold. No separate mirror opacity is necessary. A changing physical height/state changes the ray map; mixing two images or switching a source function by depth is a different operation.

For a circular end, display radius rho=R-d and source radius rho_s=R-s. At fixed d and angle theta, source and display arc lengths are rho_s dtheta and rho dtheta. Thus

    J_t=(R-s)/(R-d),       tangential magnification M_t=1/J_t.

For a general convex boundary with local curvature kappa and normal displacement before its normal coordinates intersect, J_t=(1-kappa s)/(1-kappa d). The formula follows because an inward offset of a boundary arc changes its arc length by factor 1-kappa d. The normal formula is exact for a circle. If Delta also varies along the boundary, derivatives of that dependence produce shear; include them, rather than rotating a purely normal vector by hand. For a general ray map use the full 2x2 J and its singular values.

At R=70 and s=23, d=8 gives J_t=47/62=0.7581, M_t=1.319; d=16 gives J_t=47/54=0.8704, M_t=1.149. Corresponding angular features on the two plateau rings must have display arc-spacing ratio 62/54=1.148. If independent outline fitting gives R=72, use the actual formula (ratio 64/56=1.143); do not refit s to rescue this prediction. This is prediction PRED-1, tested by E5.

For a monotone top cap h'>=0 with h,g>=0, the direct bottom-transmitted Delta is nonnegative. The held model reports Delta=0.3d-0.29W<0 on 0<=d<0.5W. No adjustment of positive n or thickness changes that sign. A negative-slope surface or a different optical path/source transform is required. This conditional rejection already follows algebraically if the measured sign survives E3.

At d=0.5W, the current heldSource jumps from s=0.36W to s=0.5W: Delta s=0.14W, or 9.16 px for R=109,W=65.4 and 9.58 px for R=114,W=68.4. A smooth single ray branch meeting a fixed source plane transversely has continuous q because refraction and intersection are continuous away from critical/path-switch points. A fold does not change this. But a raster seam does not prove a mathematical discontinuity: a continuous transition of width w could conceal it. For w<=2 px it must cover the approximately 9–10 px extra source interval and has average ds/dd at least about 5.5–6, including the ordinary background slope. E3 tests this with pixel integration and the physical branch constraints; it never equates a missing bright line with a proven discontinuity.

**6. Dispersion derived, and a new high-value prediction.** Let n vary with wavelength, with normal dispersion n_B>n_G>n_R. For a fixed source landmark s*, implicit differentiation of s(d,n)=s* gives

    delta d = -(partial s/partial n) delta n / J_n.

This is image-coordinate separation. The shader's delta source coordinate is a different quantity. In a compressed band with J_n=1.3–1.6 the same source separation is reduced in the displayed image. Near a fold it can be enlarged. For any source texture I(p)=B(q(p)), channel intensity changes satisfy delta I approximately gradient(B) dot delta q, whereas the screen derivative is gradient_p I=J^T gradient(B). Dividing channel difference by screen slope is legitimate only with the correct Jacobian, equal gains and channel profiles, and away from a fold.

There is a further sign prediction absent from the earlier reassessment. For P=h'>0, theta=atan(P)-asin(P/(n sqrt(1+P²))) increases with n. Both k_t and the exiting angle increase with n on this direct valid branch, so partial s/partial n>0. On the sheet's mirrored branch J_n<0, normal dispersion therefore predicts d_B>d_R: blue deeper inside than red. On an outward-bending negative-slope direct branch P<0, partial s/partial n<0; on the held positive-J band it again predicts d_B>d_R. FINDINGS 1b's examples instead give d_R/d_G/d_B=13/8/3 at s≈72 and 12/6/0 at s≈74. FINDINGS 19 places blue's bar-edge copy outside red's. If these are matched transmitted landmarks, both contradict the ordinary-dispersion version of these simple branches.

This is PRED-2: for the simple surface class, the sign of d_R-d_B must be nonpositive on those branches. It is cheap to kill that claim with E2. It does not predict the magnitude without a recovered index spread. If data favor the opposite sign across both effects, a shared signed *effective* dispersion can reproduce it, but negative n_B-n_R must not be called measured ordinary glass dispersion. Another lower-surface/path construction is a competing explanation, subjected to the same joint tests. Do not secretly change channel order by role.

For a modest spectral family use n(lambda)=n_G+B(1/lambda²-1/lambda_G²), with nominal lambda_R=0.610, lambda_G=0.550, lambda_B=0.460 micrometers. These are effective RGB sampling wavelengths, not measured display spectra. B>=0 is the ordinary-dispersion hypothesis; allow a separately labeled signed B only for an effective chromatic map. Actual RGB channels integrate spectra; neutral edges support effective channel mapping, not a spectral identification of the glass. Estimate a free per-channel capture gain/offset from uncovered neutral controls, not from the glass fringes being fitted.

The rim over black is a different observation. If B(q)=0 for all q, a pure transmission warp is zero at every wavelength. Bright colored light there must come from a reflection/highlight/scattering/layer contribution. The reported 16-level R-B difference divided by approximately 33 levels/px is an equivalent displacement near 0.48 px, not proof of transmission dispersion. The observed LOLL8185 encoding is yuv420p10le, PQ, BT.2020, 60 fps. Its chroma response must be calibrated. The old steps.py also pools frames, selects gradients above 40 levels and searches R/B maxima in ±6 rows around G peaks; its printed RGB triple need not be a matched feature from one frame and can be search-window limited. E2 replaces that estimator, not the underlying recording.

**7. Source ownership, two-interface extensions and the compositor alternative.** The strongest general rendering equation needed here is a sum over visible paths j and wavelengths c, followed by foreground composition:

    L_c(p)=sum_j w_j(p,c) [K_j,c * B_j,c](T_j^-1 q_j,c(p)) + R_c(p),
    Y = capture(appearance(L), coverage, foreground).

For physical incoherent transport each w_j>=0 and filters K_j have nonnegative normalized weights; transmission/reflection energies are coupled. For a deliberate image-enhancement stage signed coefficients are allowed, but it is then labeled as processing, not passive scattering. Source B_j can be raw app content, a previously composited bar, the cover sheet's wallpaper, or a separately scaled glyph layer. Its identity is not determined by where a pixel appears to be located.

A curved underside extension is defined, not left to intuition. Let the bottom be z=b(x,y), top h>b, source plane fixed below min(b). Trace v through the top with vector Snell; solve z_top+tau t_z=b(p+tau t_xy) for the smallest positive intersection before any sidewall/top intersection. At that bottom use the normal pointing into glass, N_b=(-b_x,-b_y,1)/sqrt(1+|gradient b|²), eta=n, and the same vector formula. Intersect the transmitted ray with the source plane. If transmission fails, reflect and trace; do not sample a texture at an imaginary direction. In the offline model allow at most two internal reflections, enumerate valid exit paths and include their Fresnel weights. Discard any continuation with weight below 0.005 and report its total discarded energy. If paths above that threshold remain after the bound, that model is outside the affordable class and follows the compositor fallback, not an arbitrary ray truncation presented as physics.

The small-slope curved-bottom formula explains what this extension can change. Inside slope vector is approximately a=(1-1/n)gradient h. At a bottom slope gradient b, refraction to air gives horizontal/downward ratio a_out approximately n a-(n-1)gradient b=(n-1)(gradient h-gradient b). To leading order, source displacement is

    q-p ≈ (1-1/n)(h-b) gradient h + (n-1)G(gradient h-gradient b),

where G is local vertical distance from bottom to source. A lower slope exceeding the upper slope can reverse the source displacement without a negative thickness. The full exact trace tests whether this actually works at the required angles, rather than fitting this approximation into the fold. A separately adjustable bottom profile increases model flexibility and weakens identifiability; charge its parameters in model selection.

Use the same compact basis for the bottom as for the top, with at most two additional global coefficients and one amplitude per held role. A useful bound is |b-h_flat_bottom|<=0.5R, thickness>=0.02R and no self-intersection. If only an unrestricted underside with many knots succeeds, stop the physical claim and take the effective-map branch.

The compositor fallback has two spatial contributions for the held lens: an exterior-band source and an interior bar/content source, selected or blended by a measured mask. A seam is a source-selection boundary, not a forced singular optical surface. The band can use a jointly fitted smooth ray/potential map or a shared low-order mapping. The interior begins with identity in screen-aligned bar coordinates, plus the independently measured glyph and bar transforms. Its image equation is C=(1-m) C_band + m C_interior; multiply the entire result by geometric coverage once. A discontinuous source switch may have a smooth antialiased mask. Separate its mask width from optical blur and from RGB displacement. No current 0.11 raw share is treated as an identified material constant until E4 tests it.

**8. The kernel alternatives and the 10–12 px drift.** Define a warp operator W_q B(p)=B(q(p)) and a source blur K B=integral K(v)B(p+v)dv. A source blur followed by a warp is (K B)(q(p)); blurring the warped image is K(W_q B)(p). They are not generally equal. With a zero-mean symmetric kernel of covariance Sigma and a smooth q, expansion gives source centroid

    mean q(p+v) ≈ q(p)+0.5 [trace(H_qx Sigma), trace(H_qy Sigma)].

The Fresnel formulas in section9 can be derived without using a reference as an unexplained rule. At a flat dielectric interface set incoming electric amplitude to1, reflected amplitude r, transmitted amplitude t. For s polarization, tangential electric continuity gives1+r=t; magnetic continuity gives n_i c_i(1-r)=n_t c_t t. Substitution gives r=(n_i c_i-n_t c_t)/(n_i c_i+n_t c_t). For p polarization choose the usual reflected basis, yielding c_i(1+r)=c_t t and n_i(1-r)=n_t t; hence r=(n_i c_t-n_t c_i)/(n_i c_t+n_t c_i). Its squared magnitude is section9's R_p (the amplitude sign convention does not affect intensity). Transmitted power is T=(n_t c_t)/(n_i c_i)|t|² and conservation gives R+T=1 for real lossless indices. Average the two squared amplitudes for unpolarized light. Absorption follows dL/dell=-mu L, integrating to L(ell)=L(0)exp(-mu ell). These assumptions exclude coherent thin-film interference and polarization measurements; the corpus does not resolve those additional mechanisms.

For the candidate virtual environment use E(r)=E0+A_top exp(k_top(r dot l_top-1))+A_bottom exp(k_bottom(r dot l_bottom-1)), where r is the reflected unit ray, E0/A are nonnegative RGB radiances, l_top=(0,-sin eta,cos eta), l_bottom=(0,sin eta,cos eta), eta in[15,85] degrees, and k in[1,100]. Shared eta and k are fitted across roles; start with equal top/bottom amplitudes, split them only if E8's asymmetric profiles require it. A lobe with half-width angle alpha has k=ln2/(1-cos alpha), obtained by setting its exponential to1/2. At most one scalar roughness in[0,0.3] rad broadens both lobes, or absorb that width into k and declare roughness/environment unidentifiable. Do not fit k and roughness independently when only their combined width is observed. Bound output environment contribution to at most1 normalized display unit after the appearance transform. For absorption use mu R in[0,2]; larger apparent opacity belongs first to the explicit tone/compositing stage. Normal-incidence Fresnel at n=1.5 is0.04; a measured +60-level line is not itself reflectance0.235 because the environment intensity is unknown.

To compute map derivatives without unstable finite differencing near a fold, differentiate ray intersections implicitly. For F(tau,p)=z0(p)+tau t_z(p)-b(p+tau t_xy(p))=0,

    F_tau = t_z - gradient b dot t_xy,
    d tau/dp_i = -F_pi/F_tau,
    d p_exit/dp_i = e_i + tau d t_xy/dp_i + t_xy d tau/dp_i.

Apply the same chain rule to the final plane intersection and Snell expression. F_tau=0 is a grazing intersection; it is not a reason to silently use an enormous derivative. Track the competing path and integrate finite pixels there. For flat-bottom scalar geometry, let A=(n-1)(h/n+g). Then Delta=A h' in the paraxial limit; differentiating yields Delta'=A h''+(n-1)(h')²/n, which produces section5's J_n. For arbitrary height use exact vector Snell with P=|gradient h| and inward ray direction gradient h/P; handle P=0 by zero shift. The planar-bottom closed form remains valid for a locally arbitrary top normal, provided the ray actually reaches that bottom first.

The tangential formula is part of the coordinate map, not an extra stretch applied after refraction. Let a convex boundary be b(l), tangent t(l), inward normal m(l), and m'=-kappa t. A display point is p=b+d m, so p_l=(1-kappa d)t. Its normal-map source is q=b+s m, giving q_l=(1-kappa s)t+s_l m. Therefore the local Jacobian in tangent/normal bases is

    J = [[(1-kappa s)/(1-kappa d), 0],
         [s_l/(1-kappa d),          s_d]].

For s_l=0 it is diagonal. Its off-diagonal entry is the shear missing from an independent normal displacement with an invented corner radius. For a general displacement q=p+gradient Psi the Jacobian is I+Hessian(Psi), symmetric, so mixed derivatives and loop circulation vanish. Exact nonparaxial/thick-surface maps need not satisfy that approximation; E9 distinguishes them. At a medial axis normal coordinates overlap, so none of these denominators may be clamped to hide a singularity; use the global scalar height construction below.

Curved-bottom M2 is fixed as b/R=a_b[1-f_b(d/(beta R))], with f_b having the same integral form as f but two global coefficients c_b1,c_b2 in[-8,8]. Initially a_b=0 for sheet/rest and a_b in[-0.5,0.5] for held; only the M3 role split can add other role amplitudes. The source plane is z=-gamma R; enforce b+gamma R>=0.02R wherever an exit is used, h-b>=0.02R, and first-intersection validity. M1's signed held amplitude range is[-3,tau-0.02], with its exact top/sidewall path checked. A physical source-switch hypothesis must specify whether a ray hits bar content, page content or a reflected environment; different targets cannot be swapped merely to improve a pixel.

Use translation and log-scale components for X, not an uncontrolled blend of arbitrary matrices.

The spring derivation also fixes what its fitted parameters can mean: characteristic polynomial r²+2 zeta omega r+omega²=0 has double root -omega at zeta=1, hence y=(A+B t)e^(-omega t); y(0)=1,y'(0)=0 gives A=1,B=omega. With measured nonzero v0, B=v0+omega instead. This is why one duration does not determine stiffness without the state/initial-condition assumptions.
